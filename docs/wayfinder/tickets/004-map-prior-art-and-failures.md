# Map prior art, products, and abandoned attempts

## Question

What academic systems, commercial products, open-source projects, and patents
have attempted finger-driven keyless text entry, and what does their survival
or abandonment reveal?

Build a dated landscape that separates shipped products from demos, papers,
crowdfunding campaigns, patents, and inactive repositories. Record interaction
method, hardware, layout, claimed and independently measured performance,
availability, price, last meaningful activity, failure or abandonment evidence,
and source quality. Trace especially close descendants or alternatives to
LapCal. Do not infer a failure reason when the source does not state one.

The result must identify reusable ideas, repeated failure modes, and claims that
need replication rather than repetition.

## Resolution

Resolved in the
[prior-art landscape](../../research/prior-art-landscape.md). The landscape
separates spatial QWERTY, finger-sequence QWERTY, chorded input, reduced
fingertip layouts, projected or instrumented surfaces, and adjacent gesture
controllers. It records comparable metric boundaries, current availability,
repository and community evidence, and strict status claims. Gest is the only
confirmed shutdown found; AirType/Noki and SelfieType are silent but not
confirmed cancelled, while Tap remains active through a product pivot.

The decisive precedent is TypeAnywhere: finger identity plus language can be
fast without sensing key position, but that contract is not arbitrary key
identity. True spatial QWERTY still requires a geometry channel or an
instrumented surface. The report carries that fork and the repeated correction,
calibration, environment, wearability, and productization failures into the
remaining metric and architecture tickets.
